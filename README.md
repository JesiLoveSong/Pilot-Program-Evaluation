# Pilot-Program-Evaluation

**Project Overview**
---

This project evaluated the inaugural year of a student success pilot program to assess participation, academic outcomes, student experiences, and overall program effectiveness. I collected and analyzed student performance and program participation data, conducted trend analyses, and presented findings to senior leadership to support decisions regarding future program investment, refinement, and expansion.

**Question**
---

How successful was the pilot program in its inaugural year?

**Project Objectives**
---

- Measure student participation, retention, and completion within the pilot program.
- Evaluate academic performance and enrollment-related outcomes among program participants.
- Assess participant satisfaction and perceived value of program components.
- Determine whether the program achieved its intended objectives during its first year.
- Provide data-informed recommendations to support future program development and scaling efforts.

**Data Sources**
---

- Student enrollment records
- Academic performance data
- Transfer student outcomes
- Program participation records
- Student satisfaction survey responses
- Program completion and engagement metrics

**Methodology**
---

**Data Collection and Preparation**

- Collected student academic performance, enrollment, and program participation data.
- Consolidated student records from multiple institutional reporting sources.
- Verified program eligibility, participation, and completion statuses.
- Reviewed survey responses measuring participant satisfaction and perceived program value.

**Data Analysis**

- Conducted trend analyses examining participation, completion, and academic outcomes.
- Evaluated transfer shock among program participants.
- Assessed retention and enrollment-related indicators.
- Analyzed participant satisfaction data to identify program strengths and areas for improvement.
- Compared outcomes across participant groups to better understand factors associated with successful completion.

**Reporting and Visualization**

- Developed visualizations and summary reports using Canva and Excel.
- Presented findings and recommendations to senior leadership and program stakeholders.

**Tools Used**
---

- Excel
- Tableau
- Canva
- SPSS

**Key Findings**
---

- Identified 241 eligible students, of whom 128 accepted the invitation to participate. Of those who accepted, 47 completed all program requirements, while 81 did not complete the program.
- 17% of students changed majors by the end of the Fall 2022 semester, with 55% of those changes involving majors outside the College of Business.
- Transfer shock was a significant academic challenge, with 139 students experiencing transfer shock and approximately 75% of those students experiencing major transfer shock.
- 89% of program completers reported satisfaction with program requirements, 91% expressing confidence in applying what they learned, and 91% indicating they were likely to recommend the program.
- Career coaching and scholarship funds were key program strengths.
- 63% of survey respondents worked during the semester.

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Given the prevalence of transfer shock, implement targeted academic support services during students' first semester, including academic coaching, tutoring, and transition workshops.
- Because a majority of respondents reported employment obligations, explore flexible participation options, alternative scheduling, and virtual engagement opportunities to improve completion rates.
- Maintain and potentially expand career coaching and scholarship opportunities, as participants consistently identified these elements as highly valuable.
- Monitor participant outcomes beyond the inaugural year to assess long-term impacts on academic performance, retention, graduation, and career readiness.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

- As a pilot-year evaluation, the analysis reflects outcomes from a single cohort and may not fully represent future program performance.
- The absence of long-term outcome data limited the ability to assess whether observed benefits persisted over time.
- Student satisfaction results were based on self-reported survey responses and may be influenced by response bias.
- While transfer shock was identified as a significant challenge, the analysis could not isolate all factors contributing to changes in academic performance.
- Program completion and academic outcomes may have been influenced by external factors not directly measured in the analysis, including employment obligations, financial circumstances, personal responsibilities, and prior academic preparation.
- Because participation was voluntary, students who elected to join the program may differ from non-participants in ways that affect academic outcomes, limiting causal conclusions regarding program effectiveness.

**Conclusion**
---

The pilot program demonstrated promising results during its inaugural year, particularly in participant satisfaction, career development support, and student confidence. Participants consistently identified career coaching and scholarship funding as valuable program components, and survey findings suggested strong perceived benefits among students who completed the program.

At the same time, the evaluation identified several opportunities for improvement, including addressing transfer shock, supporting working students, and improving participant completion rates. The findings provided leadership with evidence-based insights into program effectiveness and informed decisions regarding future program refinement, resource allocation, and potential expansion. Ultimately, the project demonstrated the value of data-driven program evaluation in assessing student success initiatives and guiding institutional decision-making.
